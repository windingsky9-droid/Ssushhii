Performance is part of the visual design.

Keep the cinematic feel without overwhelming the GPU.

A dramatic scene does not need every expensive effect enabled at maximum strength. The goal is visual hierarchy: spend GPU time where viewers actually notice it.

Reduce unnecessary draw work

Reuse geometry and materials, merge static meshes when practical, and avoid hundreds of tiny transparent objects. Transparency and overdraw can become expensive quickly.

Control post-processing

Bloom, depth effects, and full-screen passes are powerful because they affect the entire image. Keep the pass count deliberate and scale quality with viewport size or device capability.

Use levels of detail

Distant objects rarely need the same polygon density or shader complexity as foreground objects. Simplification is almost invisible when it follows perspective.

A stable 60 FPS scene usually feels more premium than a visually busier scene that stutters.
